# Household weather, news and optional email

Ayan's workstream, November 8, 2026. Household sharing is separate from email delivery. Ayan explicitly said not to worry about email setup; no sender was connected and no real email was sent. All delivery defaults off. Existing customer contact consent is not alert consent.

## Weather and local news

The authenticated household context endpoint uses the registered live devices' stored home locations. It excludes exercise and disabled devices, and checks household authority before and after source reads. No street address or coordinates are returned by this endpoint. NWS receives the coordinates required for its point query. Its privacy implications belong in the site's privacy disclosure.

NWS `/alerts/active?point=lat,lon` supplies official active alerts for that point, including zone-based alerts. Only actual, unexpired, non-cancelled alerts are displayed. This does not treat a state-wide declaration as a house-specific warning. The hourly forecast follows only a validated `api.weather.gov/gridpoints/.../forecast/hourly` link from `/points`. The next 12 hours show original forecast issue/valid times, temperature, wind and precipitation chance. Forecasts older than six hours are labeled stale; a bounded two-minute issue-time tolerance covers source clock skew and forecasts generated while the request is in flight. Cached responses recheck alert expiry, forecast period end times and headline age on every read. No custom storm predictor or safety/dispatch classifier was added. An official watch can precede a storm; a forecast or headline alone does not change device risk.

Weather caches refresh at most every five minutes per rounded registered location; source failure remains unavailable/partial/stale, never a clean bill of health. Context is bounded to the first five distinct household locations, with an explicit truncation indicator. The current customer household is expected to contain devices at one home; multi-location households must heed that bound.

News is headline/link/date only, from fixed publisher RSS endpoints. North Texas: Dallas Free Press and Fort Worth Report; Austin/Central Texas: KUT; Tampa Bay/Florida: WUSF; San Diego: KPBS. Each has a bounded regional coverage box, which identifies the publisher's region, not each article's location. Headlines can cover broader topics and do not imply an event at the home. Other areas say no publisher connected. No arbitrary customer feed URL, full article scraping, AI summary, or news-derived hazard score. Feeds cache 15 minutes; headlines older than seven days are omitted. Unavailable or empty feeds are reported honestly. KERA’s former feed redirected to its new website and could not be consumed as RSS; it was replaced with the publishers’ advertised Dallas Free Press and Fort Worth Report feeds. Local source checks found KUT one recent item, WUSF and KPBS five; these are point-in-time checks, not availability guarantees.

Sources: [NWS API](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api), [NWS alerts](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-alerts), [KUT news](https://www.kut.org/news), [WUSF local/state](https://www.wusf.org/local-state), [KPBS feed](https://www.kpbs.org/index.rss), [Dallas Free Press](https://dallasfreepress.com/), [Fort Worth Report](https://fortworthreport.org/).

## Optional email implementation, disabled

Configuration requires all of `HOME_EMAIL_ENABLED=true`, `HOME_EMAIL_FROM` (bare authenticated sender address), `RESEND_API_KEY`, `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET` and `SESSION_SECRET`. No key or sender is present in the shipped examples. This implementation is prepared code, not an activated or delivered service. Do not switch it on without verifying the sender, webhook and end-to-end delivery.

`/api/customer/household-email` supports self-only preferences and verification. A private manual household invitation never verifies a mailbox. The separate verification email carries a one-use, one-hour hashed token bound to the current membership/email/account generation. Acceptance requires that same signed-in account. Verification does not opt in; device and official weather emails default off and must be selected separately. Email/account changes fail closed and require renewed access/verification. Account email changes atomically revoke old memberships and close owned households, so changing an email away and back cannot revive access. Alerts-only members can manage their own preferences without reading device status through the website.

The existing dedicated data refresh scheduler invokes a bounded sweep only when email is configured. It checks at most two oldest-due households per tick, preserves device status transitions in MongoDB and records fresh official point alerts. News and forecasts do not trigger email. More households increase time between checks; the website's ten-second status refresh is not an email latency guarantee. No normal page GET sends email. Notification planning does not infer occupied homes, medical need, damage, safe entry or dispatch.

Each event/member/preference-version has one durable outbox item. Payloads are encrypted with the existing server encryption helper and retained 30 days. Every send checks current household membership, account generation, selected link ownership, verified address, preferences and suppression. Expired events and superseded device transitions are suppressed. Delivery rechecks a current authorized device snapshot and exact transition revision, including jobs outside the current two-household sweep. Hazard observation time is unknown when the customer summary lacks it; last device contact is separately labeled, and condition age/stale wording is preserved. Weather jobs recheck the current successful point-alert set; confirmed removal supersedes them, while source failure defers them. Sends are leased; retries preserve the same payload and provider idempotency key, stop after four attempts, and never extend beyond the provider's 24-hour dedupe period (local ceiling 20 hours; event lifetime normally two hours). Per-recipient attempts are separated by one minute and accepted alerts capped at six/hour. Pending queues expire rather than flood recipients after an outage.

Provider acceptance is stored as `accepted`, never `delivered`. Only a verified delivery callback can set `deliveryStatus:delivered`. A signed bounce or complaint disables that membership's alerts. HMAC webhook signatures include raw body, message ID and timestamp, with five-minute clock tolerance. Verified unmatched callbacks retain their bounded provider ID and event type for reconciliation once a receipt exists. Callback completion and matching-generation suppression are transactional; both receipt persistence and a bounded sweep reconcile early events. Retry processing is idempotent. Once a send is already accepted by the provider it cannot be recalled; revocation stops future sends.

Emails contain a purpose-scoped unsubscribe link. Its fragment is removed from the address bar on load; an explicit button stops that household's emails without requiring sign-in. Tokens expire after one year. Regular settings remain available. Unsubscribe/preferences changes invalidate queued work immediately at its next authority check. No shared device key, pairing code, owner credentials or other recipient list is sent.

Before activation: configure the signed callback at `/api/customer/household-email-events` for `email.delivered`, `email.bounced`, `email.complained`, `email.failed`; verify sender-domain authentication and recipient consent; test one authorized recipient; check bounce/complaint behavior and retention. This is an advisory prototype, not an emergency-delivery service. Privacy/legal review and physical device validation remain separate from automated checks.

Provider references: [send API](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/emails/send-email), [24-hour idempotency](https://resend.com/changelog/idempotency-keys), [signed callbacks](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/verify-webhooks-requests).
